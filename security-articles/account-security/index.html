Account Security Basics

Essential steps to secure your Discord account — 2FA, backup codes, session management, and more.

1 Enable Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)

2FA adds a second layer of security. Even if someone gets your password, they cannot log in without the 6-digit code from your authenticator app. Go to Settings → My Account → Enable Two-Factor Authentication. Use an authenticator app (Google Authenticator, Authy) rather than SMS — SMS 2FA is vulnerable to SIM-swapping attacks.

2 Save Your Backup Codes

When you enable 2FA, Discord gives you 8 backup codes. Save these somewhere safe — a password manager, a printed note, or an encrypted file. If you lose access to your authenticator app, these codes are the only way to get back into your account. Without them, Discord Support cannot help you recover a 2FA-locked account.

3 Use a Strong, Unique Password

Your Discord password should be: at least 16 characters, unique (not used on any other site), and stored in a password manager. Use a mix of letters, numbers, and symbols. Avoid common words, birthdays, or "Discord123". A password manager like Bitwarden or 1Password can generate and remember strong passwords for you.

4 Manage Active Sessions

Go to Settings → Devices and review all active sessions. You can see every device currently logged into your account, including their location and last activity. If you see anything suspicious, click "Log Out All Known Devices" — this immediately invalidates all session tokens, including any that may have been stolen.

5 Review Authorized Apps

Settings → Authorized Apps shows every third-party application that has access to your Discord account. Remove anything you don't recognize or no longer use. A malicious app with the right permissions can read your messages, join servers, and even send messages as you.

6 Privacy Settings

Review your privacy settings carefully:

  • Server Privacy Defaults — Settings → Privacy & Safety. Disable "Allow direct messages from server members" by default for new servers.
  • Friend Request Settings — Set who can send you friend requests. "Everyone" is the least safe option.
  • Data Usage — Disable "Use data to improve Discord" and "Use data to personalize my Discord experience" if you prefer not to share usage data.

7 Keep Your Email Secure

Your Discord account is only as secure as your email. If an attacker gains access to your email, they can reset your Discord password. Enable 2FA on your email account, use a strong password, and check for unexpected login alerts.

8 Be Careful With Bots and Webhooks

When adding bots to your server, only use well-known, verified bots. Check what permissions they request — a moderation bot should not need "Administrator." Webhook URLs are secrets: anyone with the URL can send messages to that channel. Never share webhook URLs publicly.
