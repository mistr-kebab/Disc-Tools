Discord Phishing Detection

How to spot fake login pages, scam links, and social engineering before it's too late.

Common Discord Phishing Tactics

Attackers use increasingly sophisticated methods to trick Discord users. Here are the most common patterns you need to recognize:

⚠ Fake Login Pages

You receive a link claiming to be Discord. The page looks identical to the real login screen — same colors, fonts, layout. You enter your email and password, and nothing happens (or you get an "error"). Your credentials have just been sent to the attacker. Always check the URL: the real Discord login is at discord.com/login. Scammers use domains like discord-nitro.com, discordgift.xyz, dlscord.com (notice the L instead of I).

⚠ Free Nitro Scams

"Free 3 months of Nitro!" or "Claim your Nitro gift!" messages with a link. There is no free Nitro. Discord's actual promotions are always announced through the official Discord blog or in-app notifications, never through random DMs or server messages. Clicking the link leads to a phishing page that asks for your Discord login — or worse, your credit card details.

⚠ QR Code Login Theft

An attacker sends you a QR code and asks you to scan it — often framed as "verification" or "claiming a reward." That QR code is actually a Discord login QR. Scanning it grants the attacker instant access to your account. Only scan QR codes from the official Discord login screen.

⚠ "Test My Game" Scam

Someone DMs you asking to "test their new game" or "try their project." They send a file (usually .exe, .msi, or .scr). Running this file installs malware that steals your Discord token, browser passwords, and cryptocurrency wallets. Never run executables from strangers.

⚠ Fake Discord Staff / Support

Someone claims to be from "Discord Trust & Safety" or "Discord Support" and says your account has been reported. They ask you to "verify" your account by providing your password, token, or by changing your email to one they control. Discord staff will never ask for your password or token in DMs. They communicate through email from @discord.com or the official in-app system.

Red Flags — How to Spot a Scam

  • Urgency: "Act now or your account will be deleted!" Legitimate services rarely use urgent threats.
  • Too good to be true: Free Nitro, free Steam games, free cryptocurrency — if it sounds too good, it's a scam.
  • Suspicious URLs: Hover over links before clicking. Look for typos (dlscord instead of discord), wrong domains (.xyz, .tk, .cf), or unfamiliar subdomains.
  • Unexpected DMs: Random people messaging you with links or files is the most common attack vector.
  • "Verify your age" / "Verify you're human": These are almost always phishing. Discord already knows your age from your account settings.
  • Grammar and spelling: Many scams originate from non-English speaking countries. Obvious grammar errors are a red flag — but not always, as AI tools are making scams more polished.

What to Do If You Clicked a Phishing Link

  1. Change your password immediately — go to Discord Settings → My Account → Change Password.
  2. Enable 2FA — Settings → My Account → Enable Two-Factor Authentication.
  3. Log out all sessions — Settings → Devices → "Log Out All Known Devices." This invalidates any stolen tokens.
  4. Check authorized apps — Settings → Authorized Apps. Remove anything you don't recognize.
  5. Run a malware scan — Use Windows Defender or Malwarebytes if you ran any downloaded files.
  6. Contact Discord Support — Report the incident at support.discord.com.
